technique 01 · job to be done

Before the building — why anyone walks in

The session started at the front door, with the visitor's own memory. Everything after is in service of this:

“I want to play a bunch of old arcade games and re-experience what I enjoyed in my childhood — and maybe discover games I haven't played in 30 years and enjoy them again.” the core job · visitor insight

★ HALL OF THE VISITOR — peak memories

1ST

Finished Dragon's Lair start-to-finish (~45–60 min) with a crowd watching. Accomplishment + an audience — that is the exciting part.

2ND

Discovery moments — Gauntlet, Pole Position, Star Wars vector. Being INSIDE it: sitting in the cockpit, flying the X-wing, R2-D2 talking to you. That was the magic.

Design laws set at the door visitor

No guided play, no forced order. The visitor freely walks up to any machine and tries things — the crowd of machines is the content.

The busy machine. A patron stands at a cabinet mid-play, blocking it — “this one's busy right now, you can't play it yet.” The real arcade feeling of machines in use.

backcasting, continued · the first minute

The first minute IS the walk-in

“The seeking, not the playing, is the core first-visit loop.” The coach's consequence: design the room to be too big to take in at once — broken sightlines, sounds from around corners. The attract-mode soundscape becomes the seek SYSTEM: you locate the game you remember by ear before you see it.

station 01

Daze

You come in and stand inside an overwhelmed room. Too many machines, all of them alive. Dazing around is not a flaw of the room — it is the point.

station 02

Hear

Sounds come from around corners. The Pac-Man theme from one side, the Galaga theme from another. The soundscape is the seek system — memory locates what eyes haven't found.

station 03

Seek

Sightlines break. You turn, you pass busy cabinets and lit marquees, you keep listening. This stretch of not-yet-finding is the hero loop of the first visit.

station 04

Find

FINDING the cabinet you're looking for. Finding-by-memory is the first hero moment — earned, not handed out. visitor coach

technique 03 · worst possible idea — inverting the first minute

The worst room, flipped into laws

The coach proposed worst-case answers on purpose. The visitor offered them gladly — and each one inverted cleanly into a rule the room must obey.

dead inside
▶ FLIP
attract mode

The worst first minute visitor

Static images of cabinets. No lit marquees. No animation. “The room is dead inside.”

Law: NO DEAD MACHINES coach flip

Every cabinet always performs — attract mode running, marquee lit, screen moving. Motion, light and sound are life systems, not decoration. Test for v1: if a screenshot of the arcade looks static, it failed.

The worst patron visitor

A player standing at a machine not actually interacting with it — a cutout, not a person. The tells of a real player: jerky motion, slamming controls, little jumps, whole-body movement into the game.

Law: one animated system coach flip

The causal chain: game events and player inputs feed each other — game event→agent impulse→body reaction The cabinet and its player are ONE animated system. The hidden spec it exposes: every machine must run headless — playable by an agent alone — so “busy” cabinets are real games, not loops. And body language is a broadcast channel: drama readable from across the room. The near beacon.

technique 04 · kill the crown jewel

Take the cabinets away — who is still served?

visitor With cabinets gone, the rest of the arcade serves different people: pinball is physical play for a different audience; Skee-Ball, Whac-A-Mole, arcade hoops belong to the casual player; everything else — the snack bar — is a fixture that supports the arcade. The coach's reading: these widen the AUDIENCE beyond gamers — tactile players, families, casual crowds; the snack bar is the retention fixture that “turns a visit into a hangout”; casual games are the head-to-head social equalizer, where the hang-out job lives.

technique 05 · trait transfer — aimed at the snack bar and the donation

The counter that keeps the hang

SNACK BAR — v1 shape visitor

A simple donation surface: a place to leave a tip, links to crypto wallets, and a Buy Me a Coffee link. Nothing more for v1 — the jar does the talking.

Yes-and: the diegetic counter coach

Hide the rails behind the fiction. An LLM attendant takes your order — ordering a coffee IS the Buy-Me-a-Coffee click. A physical tip jar that CLINKS into the room's soundscape. Donor presence made visible in-fiction: names on the cooler, “this month's coffees = 40 hours of Galaga lit.”

The attribution device visitor

An in-cabinet INSTRUCTION CARD naming the model that created the table. A real arcade artifact — cards live behind the coin door — so the credit stays inside the fiction, and becomes something you go looking for.

“THESIS CLARIFIED: no runtime local LLMs inside the arcade — in-world behavior (patrons, snack bar, etc.) must be DETERMINISTIC. The showcase is that the arcade is BUILT with a local LLM. The artifact, not the electricity.” the visitor, mid-batch — and the consequence, by the coach: LLMs author the content and behavior CODE at build time; the runtime sim is deterministic event-driven rules (game events → reaction library). NO DEAD MACHINES and the causal-chain rule survive — just authored, not inferred.

the room's hidden engine

The workshop: a door left open

visitor Somewhere in the arcade, a door stands open; on the desk lie PLANS describing exactly how the arcade and its games were built — via the BMAD method and local models. Coach, yes-anding: the factory floor made visitable — real BMAD artifacts as in-world documents (specs, epics, story files, prompt logs, commits on the monitor, test suites running); every cabinet's instruction card pairs with its plans in its drawer — showroom ↔ workshop; plans on the desk for machines not built yet are in-world foreshadowing of featured-pod launches; the open door and still-warm chair are the thesis made physical.

The workshop's state is NOT fiction visitor decision

It reads the actual project repository for facts: which machine is being worked on, and what state it's in — BMAD spec/story progress, test status, commits. The arcade is generated from its own repo as source of truth: BMAD planning docs render as diegetic paper — spread-open plans are in-progress stories, boxed-and-dated plans are shipped, a plan under an “ON HOLD” brick is blocked; the git log is the work log on the desk monitor. ★ the thesis, resolved

Local LLMs author content and behavior at BUILD time; the runtime is deterministic performance. “The arcade is a fixed recording of what local models can build” — reproducible, offline, provable.

★ the hidden synthesis

The hero-moment audience is not simulated. The room is deterministic-alive so real visitors can be the crowd. Patrons set the stage; humans fill it.

★ what to build first

Twenty-two cabinets on the wall timeline, four shrine anchors at the back, Moon Patrol already standing in the repo, fifteen tables in the Hall of Models, and an open workshop door reading the truth. Everything else is found, never announced.
